The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History says developer Saul Biskind opened the Great Northern Shopping Center in 1958 near Lorain Road and Brookpark Road Extension, and that Great Northern Mall followed in 1976. Stores and restaurants came to line Lorain Road and Great Northern Boulevard.

This page is for businesses in that district. For a home in North Olmsted, start with our North Olmsted plumbing page. Call (440) 468-0237 to talk about a commercial problem.

A district that is changing

In April 2026 Cleveland 19 reported that the mall was still operating but much quieter, with some storefronts closed. The city had changed the zoning to allow housing as well as retail, and the mayor said a grocery store might move into a vacant property. No project timeline was given.

For a business, change means tenant spaces being refit and buildings being reused. Plumbing questions follow: what the existing lines can handle, who owns them and what a new use will require.

Tenant and landlord: who handles which plumbing

Many Great Northern businesses lease space in a larger building. The lease usually divides responsibility between tenant and landlord, often by where a fixture or a line sits. Read it before calling for a repair.

  • Check whether the water heater and restroom fixtures are the tenant's or the landlord's.
  • Ask the property manager before opening a wall or a shared drain.
  • Photograph the problem and note the time it began.
  • Keep a written record of who authorized the work.

Restaurants and food service

Restaurants and food courts put heavy demand on drains. Grease, food scraps and busy dishwashing can slow lines that serve several tenants. Routine cleaning of kitchen drains and a clear plan for grease handling cost less than an emergency backup during service.

Our hydro jetting and drain cleaning pages explain what those services involve. Ask the property manager whether a grease interceptor serves your space and who maintains it.
