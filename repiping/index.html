Repiping means replacing the supply pipes in a house, not just fixing one leak. It makes sense when the pipes are failing throughout, such as galvanized steel narrowed by rust or copper with repeated pinholes.

It is a planned project. Call (440) 468-0237 to ask whether your house needs it, and we will explain how to check.

Signs you may need repiping

Brown or rusty water

Especially on first use in the morning, which can signal galvanized corrosion.

Low pressure at many fixtures

Mineral buildup narrows old pipe and chokes flow.

Repeated pinhole leaks

More than one in a few years suggests widespread corrosion.

Visible corrosion

Green or white crust on copper, rust on galvanized joints.

A single leak is not a reason to repipe. If it is only one leak, we repair it and check the rest. See burst pipe repair for that path.

Galvanized pipe in older houses

Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s and remained in use into the 1970s. It corrodes from the inside, narrows over decades and eventually restricts flow or leaks.

The Census survey profile shows about one in ten North Olmsted homes were built before 1950, and many more date from the 1950s and 1960s, so mixed piping is likely. A house may have galvanized nipples near the heater or original copper in other places.

How a repipe is done

1

Walk the house, map fixtures, agree on pipe routes and access points.

2

Pull the permit and schedule inspection.

3

Run new lines, connect fixtures and test pressure.

4

Restore

Close access holes and leave them ready for patching.

Most repipes are done with the family living in the house, with water off for part of the day. We tell you in advance when and for how long.

Permit and inspection

The City of North Olmsted Building Division issues plumbing permits, and the permit holder arranges inspections. Contractors must be registered with the city.

A repipe is a major job, so ask the Building Division what applies. We handle the permit and the inspection.

Before you repipe: alternatives

Spot repairs, replacing only galvanized sections or adding a pressure reducing valve can solve some problems. Tell us what you want to fix and we will say honestly whether a full repipe is needed.

If low pressure comes from the street, see water line repair. A leak detection visit can also confirm where the problem lives before you decide.

Living in the house during a repipe

Most repipes are done with the family home. Water is off for part of each day, and we tell you when. Walls get small access holes, and floors are covered to protect them.

Plan on a few days of dust and noise. Move items from the work areas, and tell us about pets and sleep schedules so we can plan around them.

Repiping is not the cure for every pressure problem

Low pressure has several causes. A failing pressure reducing valve, a partly closed main valve, a narrow service line or a municipal issue can all mimic bad house piping.

We test before recommending a repipe. A one-hour diagnosis can save a large expense. If the answer is a full repipe, you will see the test results that led us there.

Codes and permits for repiping

The Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), lists the pipe materials approved for drinking water, including copper and PEX. It also sets rules for support, fittings and testing.

In North Olmsted, plumbing work needs a permit before it begins, and contractors must be registered with the city. Ask for the permit number and the inspection date before work starts.
