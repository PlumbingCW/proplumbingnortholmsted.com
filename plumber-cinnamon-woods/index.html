A note on location

Real estate listings and neighborhood profiles disagree about the community's edges. Some of them list ZIP codes 44039, 44070 and 44138, and one search for the community returned a Cook Road address that is labeled Olmsted Township. We treat Jasmine Court and Ginger Lane as the anchor and do not draw a boundary.

If you are unsure which city or township you are in, check your tax bill. That decides which building department handles permits for your unit. If you live in Olmsted Township, that page covers it.

What listings say about the homes

A listing for a Jasmine Court home records a 1997 ranch-style condominium with a two-car attached garage, no basement, public sewer and public water, and a monthly association fee that covers landscaping and snow removal. Another listing shows a two-bedroom unit of about 1,700 square feet.

That is a small sample, but it points to a slab or crawl layout with the plumbing on one level and neighbors close by. It also points to a division of responsibility that your own documents will define.

Who owns what: read your association documents

In most condominiums the association maintains common elements and the owner maintains what is inside the unit. Exactly where that line falls varies by community, and the line often runs along a pipe or a wall. A leak from a neighbor's unit, a failed shutoff or a drain line shared by several units raises the question of who pays and who calls.

  • Read the declaration and the rules for plumbing responsibilities.
  • Know where your unit's water shutoff is, and test that it turns.
  • Tell the association promptly about a leak that may involve common lines.
  • Keep photos and dates for any claim or dispute.
