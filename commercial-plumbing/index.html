A commercial plumbing emergency means lost sales, safety risks or an inspection problem. If water is flowing, shut the main valve. If a drain is backing up, stop using fixtures and call (440) 468-0237.

We take commercial calls 24/7. Give us the business type, address, what is affected and whether you are the owner, manager or tenant.

When a commercial problem is an emergency

  • Water leaking into a ceiling or stock area.
  • Floor drain overflow or sewer backup in a kitchen or restroom.
  • No hot water in a food service or care setting.
  • A broken pipe in a rental unit with tenants affected.
  • A grease trap overflow.

Close the main shutoff and move inventory. Photograph damage and tell the property manager.

Commercial leak detection

Leaks in commercial buildings can be harder to find because of slab, ceiling and shared walls. We start with the meter, isolate sections and use acoustic and thermal tools to narrow the spot.

Our leak detection page lists the full process. For business owners, a quick test can save a large bill.

Grease traps and kitchen lines

Restaurants and food shops need grease trap service on a schedule. Overflowing traps cause odor and drain backups. The city wastewater office lists grease blockages among its emergency calls.

We clean traps, jet kitchen lines with hydro jetting and advise on schedules. Keep service records for inspections.

Backflow testing and prevention

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers on water lines, irrigation and fire systems. They must be tested on a schedule and the results kept.

The city plumbing application asks for a backflow prevention certification number, which shows it is a tracked item. We arrange testing and repairs for devices we can service.

Property managers and tenants

If you manage buildings, call with the unit, the problem and access details. Work-order style calls are fine. Tell us if the tenant must be present.

We document work and can send notes that help with insurance and owner reports. Repeated problems in one building may need a camera or repipe plan, covered by sewer inspection.

Honest scope

Commercial work covers many specialties. If a job is outside what we handle, such as large fire sprinkler systems or specialized process piping, we say so and do not pretend.

Tell us the job type when you call so we can match the right technician.

Restaurants, shops and offices: what usually goes wrong

Each kind of business has its own pattern. Restaurants fight grease in kitchen lines, floor drains that smell and hot water that runs out during service. Retail shops see restroom fixture failures and water heater leaks in back rooms.

Offices tend to have supply leaks above ceilings and toilets that run for days before anyone notices. Rental buildings add shared stacks, where one tenant's clog backs up the unit below. Knowing the pattern lets the technician bring the right equipment on the first trip.

What to have ready before you call

  • The address, the suite or unit number and the name of the person on site.
  • What is affected, such as a restroom, kitchen, floor drain or the whole building.
  • Whether water is still flowing and whether the main is shut.
  • Any prior cleaning or repair on the same line and when it happened.
  • Who can approve the work and how the invoice should be handled.

That information turns a vague emergency into a plan. It also helps with insurance reporting afterward.

Be ready before the next emergency

Walk the building once and note where the main shutoff, the water heater shutoff and the floor drains are. Label them. Post the numbers where staff can see them.

Photograph meters, valves and any known problem areas now. If a leak or backup happens later, those pictures show the before state. Business property and interruption policies differ widely, so read the terms for water damage and sewer backup rather than assuming coverage.

Codes and compliance for commercial work

Commercial plumbing in Ohio follows the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). It covers fixture counts, grease interceptors, backflow prevention and accessible cleanouts, and your local building department decides what needs a permit.

Water suppliers can also set cross-connection rules for commercial services. We ask your utility what applies before we size or replace a backflow assembly.
