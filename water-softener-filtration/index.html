Hard water leaves scale on fixtures, spots on glass and buildup in water heaters. A softener removes the minerals that cause it. A filter addresses different things, such as taste, odor or sediment.

Start with a test, not a sales pitch

Cleveland Water's 2025 report lists hardness of 105 to 130 ppm, averaging 117, which is moderately hard. That is a regional number, so test your tap and ask your utility which report applies to your street.

A simple test kit shows hardness. A lab test shows other contaminants. A system sized to your actual water works better and costs less than one chosen from a brochure.

Softener installation and repair

A softener installs on the main water line with a bypass, a drain line and an overflow. It needs a power outlet and room for salt. The drain must meet code and avoid a cross connection.

Repairs include control valves, resin tanks, brine valves and injectors. If the system is not working, we check the settings, the salt and the resin before replacing parts.

What a softener does to your plumbing

Softened water reduces scale on heating elements and in the water heater. Many tankless units benefit. See tankless water heater for flush schedules.

A softener adds sodium to the water in small amounts. If you are on a restricted diet, ask your doctor and keep an unsoftened cold tap in the kitchen.

On-demand and compact options

Some homes choose compact softeners or point-of-use systems. Space, flow and budget decide. A small house with one bath needs a smaller unit than a family of five.

We size by flow rate and hardness, not by family size alone.

Maintenance and when to replace

Salt, resin cleaning and valve service keep a softener working. Resin beads wear out over many years. Many softeners last 10 to 15 years.

An annual look catches problems early. If the system is old and failing, replacement may cost less than repairs.

Signs of hard water at home

White crust on fixtures

Scale builds on faucet aerators, showerheads and kettles.

Spots on dishes and glass

Minerals stay behind as water dries.

Soap that will not lather

Hard water reacts with soap and leaves film.

Short water heater life

Scale on the tank bottom and elements shortens service life.

These signs show hardness, not safety. If you worry about contaminants, a lab test is the right step, not a guess from spots.

Sizing a softener the right way

A softener is sized by capacity in grains between regenerations. The calculation uses hardness, household water use and how many days you want between cycles.

Oversizing wastes salt and water. Undersizing means hard water breaks through. We calculate from your test result and your number of bathrooms, and we set the regeneration schedule to match.

Drain, overflow and code details

A softener discharges brine and rinse water. That water must go to a floor drain, a standpipe or a laundry tub with an air gap so it cannot siphon back into the system.

North Olmsted prohibits clear water connections to the sanitary sewer in specific cases, such as sump pumps, so ask the Building Division if you are unsure where a discharge may go. We place the drain according to code and test for leaks before we leave.
