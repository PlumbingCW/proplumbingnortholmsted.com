Storms, sewers and basements: the Fairview Park pattern

After storms on August 24, 2023, Fairview Park residents reported 5 to 8 inches of water in basements. The mayor told local news that parts of the city's sewer system are about 100 years old, that a $3.5 million grant would fund a sewer section and that the city had bought a sewer jet cleaner in 2022.

What that means for you is that the public system has limits in a downpour. Inside your house, the parts you control are the lateral to the street, the floor drain and the sump. A clean lateral and a backwater valve reduce what a surcharged main can push back.

1950s ranches and what is inside the walls

Many Fairview Park homes date from the 1950s and 1960s. Cleveland Magazine describes starter homes with modest square footage, and the city's streets were largely platted and paved in the late 1920s.

That era brings cast iron drains, clay or cast iron laterals and original supply lines that are nearly 70 years old. A house that has had two clogs in two years should get a sewer inspection before a third backup. A cable alone rarely fixes a lateral with roots.

Before we arrive: if you have a sewer backup or a flooded basement

Stop the water

Shut off washers, dishwashers and taps, and stop flushing so you do not add to the flood.

Stay out of standing water

Keep power off at the panel only if the panel is dry. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter.

Lift belongings

Raise boxes and electronics onto shelves and photograph damage for your insurer.

Codes and permits in Fairview Park

In Fairview Park the standard is the Ohio Plumbing Code, which borrows from the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Permit rules come from the city, and an older sewer connection can prompt questions that an inspector wants answered.

Ask whether a backwater valve or a sewer repair needs a permit before you schedule it. We can help describe the work in plain terms for the permit counter.
