Sewer Camera Inspection in North Olmsted, OH

A small camera on a flexible rod shows exactly what is inside your sewer line, so repairs and purchase decisions rest on video, not guesses.

A sewer camera inspection pushes a waterproof camera through your drain line and records what it sees. You get a look at pipe material, joints, roots, buildup and damage without digging anything up.

Most calls come from two situations. One is a house that keeps backing up. The other is a buyer who wants to know what is under a 1960s ranch before closing. Call (440) 468-0237 to schedule a camera visit.

What the camera can show

  • Pipe material, such as cast iron, clay, plastic or a mix that changes along the run.
  • Roots entering at joints and how much of the pipe they fill.
  • Cracks, offset joints and sections that have collapsed.
  • Bellies, where the pipe sags and holds water and waste.
  • Scale and grease buildup that narrows the opening.

The location of a problem can also be marked at the surface using a locator, which saves digging in the wrong spot. This is useful before any sewer line repair is planned.

Inspecting a house before you buy

A home inspection usually covers visible plumbing but not the underground sewer lateral. For a house built in the 1950s through the 1970s, a camera inspection fills that gap.

Ask the seller for access to the cleanout, and schedule the camera before the inspection contingency ends. Roots or a collapsed section found now can be negotiated. The same finding after closing is yours to pay for.

What a visit looks like

The technician finds the cleanout, runs a cable if the line needs clearing first, then feeds the camera down the pipe while a monitor shows the live picture. You can watch along and ask questions.

Afterward you get a plain explanation of what the footage shows. When video is recorded, you can ask for a copy for a contractor, an insurer or a home purchase.

A note on local sewer rules

In North Olmsted the owner is responsible for the building sewer, which is the pipe from the house to the public connection. The city separates sanitary and storm sewers and prohibits tying downspouts or foundation drains into the sanitary line.

A camera can reveal an illegal connection. If you receive a city notice about a connection, the city requires a dye test performed by a registered plumber. We can inspect the line and discuss next steps.

Not every finding needs an immediate repair. We help you rank them so you spend on what matters first.

Private lateral versus public main

The camera follows your line up to the public connection and sometimes a short way beyond. Defects on your side are yours to fix. A problem in the public main is for the city to address.

If the video suggests a defect at or beyond the connection, we tell you and suggest you notify the city wastewater collection office. Documentation helps that conversation.
