Westlake housing: newer pipes, different failures

The Census survey profile for Westlake puts the median home at 1982, with about 23 percent built in the 1980s, 22 percent in the 1990s and 16 percent in the 1970s. Westlake grew fast after the highway arrived, so its houses are younger than the 1950s ranches next door in North Olmsted.

Younger houses usually have copper or PEX supply lines and plastic drains instead of galvanized steel and cast iron. That shifts the common calls toward water heaters nearing their second or third replacement, failing supply fittings, hose bibs and sump pumps. A 1990s tank heater is now past the age where we replace it before it leaks, so a water heater replacement call is common here.

Water main breaks and what they do inside your house

Westlake has had its share of main breaks. In early July 2018, Cleveland Water main breaks hit the 3000 block of Oakwood Lane and Creekside Drive. In March 2021 a 12-inch main broke at Columbia and Detroit, and a second 12-inch main broke days later at Dover Center and Hilliard. The utility's director told local TV the pipes were at least 25 years old and could be as old as 75.

A break in the street is the utility's repair, but the effects arrive inside your house. Watch for brown water, sputtering faucets and sudden low pressure afterward. Run a cold tap until it clears, and call us if pressure stays low or you see a wet spot, because a break can stress old fittings. Our water line repair page explains how we separate a street issue from a service line leak.

The 2014 flood and basements in Westlake

On May 12, 2014, the National Weather Service reported nearly 1,000 homes flooded across Olmsted Falls, North Olmsted and Westlake. Many basements that stayed dry for decades took water that night.

A working sump pump with a battery backup is the best defense. If yours is older than a decade, test it by pouring water into the pit. Our sump pump installation and repair crew replaces failed pumps at any hour.
