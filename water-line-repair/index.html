The water service line is the buried pipe that brings water from the street connection and meter to your house. When it leaks, you may see a wet yard, a spinning meter, low pressure or no water at all.

If water is surfacing in the yard or the house has lost pressure, call (440) 468-0237. We answer 24/7 and can explain how to isolate the break.

When a service line break is an emergency

No water

A broken line can drop pressure to zero. Check whether neighbors have water too.

Water surfacing

A wet patch or a bubbling spot in the yard can be a leak above the line.

Spinning meter

A meter that moves with everything off points to a leak.

Sudden low pressure

A drop across the house can mean a partial break or a failing valve.

Close the main shutoff if you can reach it. If the leak is outside, we will help you find the curb valve and tell you whether to call the water utility first.

Low water pressure: causes and checks

Low pressure is common and not always the service line. A clogged aerator, a failing pressure reducing valve or narrowed galvanized pipe can all cause it.

We test pressure at an outdoor hose bib and compare it to pressure at fixtures. If the pressure is low at the hose bib, the problem is in the service line or at the utility. If it is fine outside but weak inside, the house piping is the issue, and repiping may be the cure.

Locating a buried leak

Digging blindly is expensive. We use acoustic equipment to listen along the line and a line locator to trace its path, so the excavation is at the right spot.

Many service line leaks are found by a meter test. If the meter runs with the house main closed, the leak is in the buried section. Our leak detection page lists the full process.

Repair or replace the line

Spot repair

  • One short leak in an otherwise sound line
  • Smaller excavation
  • Lower cost
  • Does not fix an aging line

Full replacement

  • Old or repeatedly leaking line
  • New material for decades of service
  • Can be done by trenching or by pulling a new pipe
  • Higher cost, longer disruption

The decision depends on the age and material of the line and where the leak is. Houses built in the 1950s and 1960s may have galvanized or older copper services.

Who is responsible for what

Utilities typically own the main in the street and often the connection up to the meter or curb stop. The property owner usually owns the line from that point to the house. Rules vary, so ask your water utility where its responsibility ends.

Water main breaks do happen locally. A Mastick Road main break in September 2026 flooded three houses and left about 15 without water, according to local news. A main break in the street is the utility's job, a leak on your side is yours.

Permits and excavation

The City of North Olmsted requires permits for plumbing work, and a right-of-way registration is needed for work in the public right of way. Contractors must be registered with the city.

We handle the permit steps and call for utility locates before digging.
