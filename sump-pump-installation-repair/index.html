When a sump pump failure is an emergency

The danger is rising water. These signs mean act now.

  • The pit is full and the pump does not start.
  • The pump runs but water does not leave, or it runs constantly without lowering the level.
  • The power is out during a storm and there is no battery backup.
  • The float is stuck or the check valve is chattering.

Check that the pump is plugged in and that the breaker has not tripped. A GFCI outlet that tripped is a common culprit. If those are fine, call the 24/7 line.

Why North Olmsted basements need working pumps

On May 12, 2014, a flash flood put nearly 1,000 homes under water across Olmsted Falls, North Olmsted and Westlake, according to the National Weather Service. Residents also reported water coming up through basement drains in 2020.

A pump does not stop sewer backup, but it handles groundwater. Pairing a reliable pump with a backwater valve on the sanitary line addresses both. See sewer line cleaning for the sanitary side.

What the city requires of sump pumps

Ordinance 1339.01 requires a secure cover on the sump pit. The city also bars storm water connections to the sanitary sewer, and its backwater valve application asks where your sump pump discharges, front, rear, side or street storm sewer.

If the city issues a notice, a registered plumber must perform a dye test of every downspout, sump pump, French drain and footer tile. We make sure the discharge goes outside, not into the sanitary line.

Sewage ejector pumps are different

If a basement bathroom uses an ejector pump, that pump moves sewage, not groundwater. It needs a sealed pit and a vent and a different maintenance plan.

If you smell sewage near a basement bath, tell us. Related drainage questions are covered on basement waterproofing.

Downspouts, grading and drain tile

A pump works harder when roof water and grading send water toward the house. The city has a chapter on grading and surface water drainage, and downspouts should discharge away from the foundation, not into the sanitary sewer.

We check the discharge line, the check valve and the outside outlet. If it ends too close to the house, water cycles back into the pit.

What to do before we arrive when the pump fails

  1. Check that the pump is plugged into a working outlet and the breaker has not tripped.
  2. Lift the float by hand to see if the pump starts. Unplug it first if the water is deep.
  3. Bail or wet-vac only if the floor is dry and you are not standing in water near electrical outlets.
  4. Move stored items off the floor and photograph the water line for your insurer.
  5. Keep the cover on the pit in place once you are done, as the city code requires.
