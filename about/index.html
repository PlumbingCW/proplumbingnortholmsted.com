Licensed and insured

Plumbers sent from our line are licensed and insured. North Olmsted also requires contractors to register with the city and to show a state license and proof of liability insurance before permits are issued.

You can check any Ohio plumbing contractor with the state licensing board. We encourage it. A real plumber will not mind the question.

Why local knowledge matters

North Olmsted's housing is mostly postwar. The Census survey profile puts the median home at 1966, and about 83 percent of homes predate 1980. Those houses have cast iron, clay and old copper in places, and many have basements with sump pumps.

The city also has its own wastewater plant and its own rules on downspouts, sump pumps and backwater valves. Knowing those rules saves time on permits and on diagnosis.

Pricing and honesty

We give a clear price before work starts. We do not promise arrival windows we cannot keep, and we will tell you when a repair is not worth doing.

If a problem is outside what we handle, we say so. See the services we cover.

What we handle

Our work falls into three groups. Emergency repairs include burst pipe repair, sewer line cleaning, failed water heaters and flooded basements. Diagnostic work covers leak detection and sewer inspection with cameras. Planned work covers tankless water heaters, repiping and fixture installs.

Each of those has its own page with the signs to watch for, the factors that change the price and the questions to ask any plumber. Read the page that matches your problem before you call, then call and describe what you see.

What to expect when a plumber arrives

  1. The plumber confirms the problem with you and looks at it.
  2. You are told what is wrong, what the options are and what each costs.
  3. Work begins only after you agree to the price.
  4. The repair is tested, and the plumber explains what was done.
  5. You get a clear record of the work for your files and your insurer.

If the diagnosis shows a bigger problem than the one you called about, you hear that before anything else happens. You can say no and still keep the shutoff advice we gave on the phone.

How we handle emergencies

A plumbing emergency is any problem that is causing damage right now or that makes the house unsafe to use. Water that will not stop, sewage in the basement, a failed water heater leaking onto a finished floor and a sump pump that quit during a storm all qualify.

Our first job on the phone is to help you limit the damage. We walk you to the main shutoff, tell you whether to switch off power to wet areas and explain what to photograph for your insurer. Then we dispatch a technician.

What we will not do

We do not make up arrival times, publish prices that cannot be true for every house or promise results that depend on conditions we have not seen. We also do not recommend work you do not need.

If a repair is not worth the money, such as a very old water heater with a rusted tank, we will say so and explain the options. That honesty is the reason people call us a second time.
