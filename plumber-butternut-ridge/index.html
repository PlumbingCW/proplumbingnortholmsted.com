Butternut Ridge is the oldest part of the story of North Olmsted. The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History says David Johnson Stearns settled on Butternut Ridge in 1815, and it describes the historic district along Butternut Ridge Road between Lorain and Columbia Roads as home to some of the city's oldest homes.

If you own one of those houses, this page explains how we think about plumbing in older, one-of-a-kind homes. Call (440) 468-0237 to ask about a specific problem.

About the district

Residents describe Butternut Ridge Road as the only designated historic district in North Olmsted, running from Columbia Road to Lorain Road. The city has not given us a published boundary, so we describe the area by the road itself. Local news coverage calls many of its houses century homes kept by long-term owners.

Houses on the road look different from one another. That variety is part of the appeal and is also why we do not use a standard plan. Every call starts with the house in front of us.

Plumbing in a house that has been changed over time

An older house is rarely original. Kitchens get moved, baths get added and a furnace room becomes a laundry. The result is often plumbing from several decades in one building, with new fixtures connected to old runs.

That mix is worth documenting. When a wall or floor is open, ask the plumber to note the pipe materials, where lines were joined and where shutoff valves are. Photos of that work help the next repair. We do not assume any particular material is in your house until we see it.

  • Locate and exercise every shutoff valve, including the main.
  • Look under sinks and around the water heater for stains or mineral crust.
  • Note any sections of pipe that were added later and who did the work.
  • Keep receipts and permit paperwork for every plumbing change.

Questions to ask before work starts

Historic setting adds a few questions. Ask the North Olmsted Building Division whether any exterior work, such as a vent termination, a new service line or a regraded yard, needs extra review in the historic district. Ask the plumber whether a repair can be done without removing original finishes inside.
