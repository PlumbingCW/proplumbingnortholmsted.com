The main sewer line carries everything from your house to the public sewer at the street. When it clogs, the lowest drain in the house is the first to show it, usually a basement floor drain or a tub.

If you see rising water now, stop using fixtures and call (440) 468-0237. We take main line calls 24/7 and can usually tell on the phone whether it is a cleanout job or something worse.

When a main sewer backup is an emergency: what to do first

Locate your cleanout if you know where it is. It is a capped pipe, often in the basement near the foundation wall or outside near the house. Do not open it while water is rising, because pressure can push sewage out.

Shut off the washer and stop flushing. If the backup is already on the floor, keep power cords up and out of the water. Our drain cleaning page lists signs that separate a branch clog from a main line one.

Where main line clogs come from

Roots

Tiny roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper.

Grease and wipes

Fat, so-called flushable wipes and paper towels bind together downstream.

Scale and sag

Old cast iron narrows from rust, and a sagging section holds water and solids.

Foreign objects

Toys, rags and construction debris occasionally find their way into laterals.

How the cleaning is done

We start at the cleanout with a large cable, which cuts through most blockages and restores flow. If the cable returns with roots or grease, we follow with jetting to wash the pipe wall. A camera pass confirms the pipe is clear and shows any damage.

That sequence solves the immediate emergency and answers the follow-up question, which is whether the clog will return. If the pipe is damaged, cleaning only buys time and the next step is sewer line repair.

Sewer drain cleaning on a schedule

A line with a history of root growth benefits from planned cleaning before it backs up. Many homeowners pick an interval after the first emergency, and the right spacing depends on what the camera shows.

A scheduled visit costs less than an after-hours call and avoids a flooded basement. We will tell you honestly if the pipe has deteriorated to the point where cleaning is a short-term patch.

The 1960s lateral and what it tells us

Houses built from the 1950s through the 1970s make up nearly three quarters of North Olmsted housing, based on Census survey data. Their sewer laterals are often clay or cast iron with many joints, and each joint is a place roots can enter.

That does not mean your line is failing. It does mean a camera look is a smart purchase if you have had two backups, because it tells you whether to keep cleaning or plan a repair. Related reading on hydro jetting explains why jetting often follows a cable.

Storm water, backwater valves and the city sewer

Cleaning your private line will not stop a backup that comes from an overloaded public sewer in a storm. North Olmsted has a backwater valve policy and permit through the Engineering Department, and the city says a valve needs a cleanout on each side. The dye test that proves no downspout or footing drain feeds the sanitary line must be done by a registered contractor.

If you have flooded more than once in heavy rain, ask about a backwater valve. We can install one and make sure a downspout or sump pump is not feeding the sanitary line, since that is prohibited by city code.
