A sewer line fails slowly, then all at once. Roots work into a joint, a section of clay shifts, cast iron rusts through, and one day the main stops draining. If sewage is coming up in your basement, stop water use and call (440) 468-0237.

The 24/7 line takes sewer calls at any hour. We first restore flow if we can, then use a camera to find out whether the pipe needs cleaning, a spot repair or replacement.

Emergency signs the problem is the sewer line, not a drain

Multiple fixtures back up

Tub, toilet and floor drain acting up together means the shared main is blocked.

Cleaning does not last

Cabled twice and still clogging points to a damaged pipe or roots re-entering.

Soggy patch in the yard

A wet, sunken or unusually green strip over the lateral can mean a leaking sewer.

Sewer smell indoors

A dry trap or a cracked pipe lets gas into the house.

Any one of these is a reason to run a camera. Do not wait for a full backup, because sewage damage costs far more than a diagnosis. Our sewer inspection page covers the camera visit.

Cracked pipe with root intrusion: the spot repair question

The most common sewer diagnosis we hear described is one cracked section with roots growing through it. The rest of the pipe is fine and the homeowner wants to fix only the bad part.

A spot repair is often possible when the camera shows a short damaged section and the pipe on either side is sound. Options include a lined patch from inside the pipe or a small excavation to cut out and replace the section. The right choice depends on depth, location and what sits above the pipe.

Trenchless or dig: how the options compare

Trenchless lining or patching

  • Little yard damage, no trench along the whole run
  • Works when the pipe still holds its shape
  • Seals joints against roots
  • Not suited to collapsed or badly offset pipe

Open excavation

  • Replaces the pipe completely
  • Fixes collapsed, bellied or offset sections
  • Can reroute around obstacles
  • Disturbs lawn, drive or walkway

The camera decides. We will show you the footage and explain why one method fits your line, not the other. If there is a belly, where water pools in a low spot, lining alone will not fix it.

Cast iron, clay and plastic: what is under your house

Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s often have cast iron inside the foundation and clay or cast iron from the house to the street. North Olmsted's median house dates to 1966, so these materials are common in the city.

Cast iron rusts from the inside and flakes. Clay is brittle and joints open as the ground moves. Each has different repair rules. Pipe breaks inside the house are a separate job from the underground lateral, and our burst pipe repair page covers them.

Basement flooding and sewer repair are not always the same problem

Heavy rain can overload the public sewer. North Olmsted's wastewater office describes wet-weather overflows and has built storage and a relief sewer on Walter Road and in the Deerfield area to address them. Sewage that enters through your floor drain during a storm may be a public system issue, not a broken lateral.

The city also requires owners to keep roof drains and foundation drains off the sanitary sewer, and an illegal connection can send storm water into your line. We check both the pipe and the connections. Your sump pump discharge should go outside, not into the sewer.

If your home is on a septic system

Most homes in North Olmsted connect to the city sewer. If your property is on a septic system, tell us when you call. Septic work follows different rules and needs a different technician.
