Hydro jetting sends water through a hose at very high pressure to scrub the inside of a drain pipe. It clears grease, scale, sludge and root hair that a cable leaves behind, which is why it is the usual answer when a clog keeps returning.

If sewage is backing into a basement now, stop using water and call (440) 468-0237. We take jetting calls 24/7, and a technician will tell you over the phone whether jetting or a simple cable is the right first move.

When a sewer backup is an emergency: what to do first

A backup at 2 a.m. feels like a crisis because it is one. Sewage in a floor drain or tub carries bacteria, so the first jobs are to stop adding water and protect people and belongings.

  1. Stop every tap, toilet, dishwasher and washer in the house.
  2. Move stored items and electronics off the basement floor.
  3. Keep children and pets out of the wet area and wear boots and gloves if you must enter.
  4. Do not pour chemicals into the drain or run a pressure washer into it.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us how many drains are affected.

Photograph the water and any damaged belongings before you clean. If an insurance claim is possible, those pictures help, and you can read about coverage on our drain cleaning page.

Why the clog keeps coming back

A cable is a spinning blade on a flexible cord. It cuts through a clog, but it leaves a ring of grease and scale clinging to the wall, and that ring catches the next load of debris.

People often tell us the last company snaked the line and it clogged again within weeks. Often the cable only opened a channel the width of the cable while the pipe was still mostly narrowed. Jetting washes the full diameter, so flow returns to what the pipe was built for.

The honest answer is that jetting is a cleaning tool, not a repair tool. If the line is physically broken, the camera will show it and we will tell you so before any digging is discussed. See sewer line repair for those cases.

Camera first, jet second, camera again

A good jetting job has three steps. A camera confirms the pipe is sound and finds where the blockage sits. The jetter then clears it. A second pass with the camera shows the pipe wall after cleaning and records what remains.

That order matters on older lines. Clay and cast iron from the 1950s and 1960s can have hairline cracks that high pressure will worsen if nobody checked first. Our sewer inspection page explains the video, and you can keep the footage.

Who owns which part of the line in North Olmsted

North Olmsted has its own wastewater treatment plant, and the city collection crew answers overflows on public sewers. The pipe from your house to the street connection is the owner's responsibility under the city code, which assigns the cost of the building sewer to the owner.

That split decides who you call. If only your house backs up, the jetting job is on your line. If neighbors on the street also have sewage in basements, call the city wastewater office first, then call us for a cleanout so your line is clear when flow returns.

Jetting for restaurants, rentals and shared lines

Grease lines at food businesses and older rental buildings with shared stacks are common jetting jobs. Property managers can call on behalf of tenants and should give the unit, the fixtures affected and any prior cleanings.

A building with a grease trap needs the trap cleaned as well as the line, which is part of commercial plumbing work.

Standards and limits we work within

Jetting is done with equipment matched to the pipe size and material, and a camera look tells us whether the pipe can take it. A cracked or collapsed pipe should be repaired, not jetted.

Jetting normally starts at a cleanout. The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the Ohio Plumbing Code follows, calls for access cleanouts on building sewers, and a house without one may need one added first.
