What we know about the housing

The public record we found is thin. One listing for a home on Pheasants Walk Drive records a 1989 colonial with three bedrooms, three baths, an unfinished basement and an irrigation system, served by public sewer and public water. That fits a late 1980s subdivision, but it is a single house.

Because the neighborhood has no published boundary, we describe it by that street. Homes in the late 1980s were built after older plumbing materials had largely given way to copper and plastic, but we still look at each house before saying what is in it.

More bathrooms and a bigger demand on the water heater

A three-bath colonial puts more load on a water heater than a small ranch. Showers that run together, a laundry room and a dishwasher can all draw at once. A tank that is past ten to fifteen years of age, the range the U.S. Department of Energy gives for storage units, deserves a check even when it still heats.

If hot water runs out sooner than it used to, the cause can be sediment, a failing element or burner, or simply a unit that no longer matches the household. Our water heater repair and water heater replacement pages explain how each decision works.

Outdoor lines and seasonal care

The listing mentions an irrigation system. Systems like that connect to the home's water supply and are drained or blown out before freezing weather. A broken outdoor line can leak for weeks without showing indoors.

  • Watch the water meter with every fixture off to catch a slow leak.
  • Shut off and drain outdoor lines before the first hard freeze.
  • Disconnect hoses from outside spigots in fall.
  • Check the basement ceiling below bathrooms for stains.
